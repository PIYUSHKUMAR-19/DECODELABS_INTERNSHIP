Project Overview

This project was completed as part of the Data Analytics Project 4  under the DecodeLabs Industrial Training Program (Batch 2026).

The project focuses on Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) to transform a raw transactional dataset into meaningful business insights. The primary objective is to investigate the dataset systematically, identify important patterns and trends, understand distributions, detect potential outliers, and summarize findings that can support data-driven decision-making.

Rather than focusing only on reporting numbers, this project follows an analytical approach to uncover the story hidden within the data through descriptive statistics, comparative analysis, trend analysis, segmentation, and interactive visualization. This aligns with the project's objective of moving from static data toward meaningful analytical insights.

🎯 Project Objective

The main objective of this project is to perform a structured Exploratory Data Analysis of the available transactional data and answer key business questions such as:

What is the overall sales and order performance?
What are the mean, median, and count-based statistics?
Which products contribute the most to sales?
How does sales performance change over time?
Which customer and marketing segments show stronger performance?
What is the distribution of order values?
Are there high-value orders or potential outliers?
What proportion of orders falls into different operational statuses?
Which patterns or relationships can provide useful business insights?

The overall goal is to move beyond simple reporting and use analytical reasoning to identify patterns, trends, distributions, relationships, and anomalies in the dataset.

Average Order Value	Measures average value per order

📌 Important Analytical Note

The dashboard focuses on observed relationships and patterns within the dataset. Findings such as channel performance or order-status patterns should not automatically be interpreted as causal relationships unless additional information—such as marketing spend, margins, refund costs, acquisition costs, or operational lead times—is available.

This distinction helps ensure that the analysis remains data-driven and does not claim conclusions that the available data cannot support.
